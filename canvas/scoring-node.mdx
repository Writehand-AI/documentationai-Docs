description: "Productised's answer to conditional logic. Instead of building branching rules, you describe the segments you care about — and every respondent is classified and given a matched outcome."
---

Most form builders give you **conditional logic**: hand-written *if/then* rules that branch the questions someone sees and the outcome they get. The Scoring Node replaces that entirely with a different model — you describe the **segments** you care about, and the product classifies each respondent and tailors their outcome accordingly.

Without scoring, everyone who completes your product gets roughly the same output, personalised only by the details they shared. With scoring, the outcome changes based on *who they are* — a high-fit lead gets a different report, a different call to action, and a different next step than someone who isn't ready yet.

## How it works

The Scoring Node sits between your AI Product and your Outcome node: **AI Product → Scoring → Outcome**. It must connect *directly* to the AI Product node.

The moment the conversation ends — before the outcome is generated — the Scoring Node:

1. Reads the **full collected conversation** plus any deterministic scores (see below).
2. Compares it against the **segments** you defined, using your **objective** as the lens.
3. Assigns the single **best-fit segment** and writes a short rationale.
4. Passes that segment to the Outcome node, where its **output instruction** shapes what gets generated.

The respondent never sees the scoring logic — only the outcome it produces.

---

## Two scoring layers that work together

### 1. Deterministic scores (set per question)

This is the predictable, auditable layer. In your **AI Product's question editor**, any **scale**, **yes/no**, or **choice** question can have scoring switched on:

- **Enable scoring** on the question.
- **Points per answer** — e.g. *Yes = 10, No = 0*, or a point value for each choice option. Scale questions use the number directly (7/10 = 7 points).
- **Weight** — a multiplier so some questions count more than others.

These combine into per-dimension scores and an overall percentage with status bands (low / medium / high). It's pure arithmetic — the same answers always produce the same score — and it's what fills score tokens like `{{ object:overall_score }}` on your result pages.

<Callout kind="tip">
Use deterministic scoring whenever the result gates a real decision — lead qualification, readiness gating, compliance thresholds. Because the maths is fixed, you can audit exactly how any individual was scored and explain it to a prospect or client.
</Callout>

### 2. Segment classification (the Scoring Node)

This is the judgement layer. The node takes everything — the conversation *and* the deterministic scores — and decides which segment the person belongs to. You choose how it decides:

- **Automatic (AI decides)** — the AI reads the whole conversation holistically and picks the best-fit segment from your descriptions. Best for qualitative signals that are hard to reduce to numbers, and for getting live quickly.
- **Rule-based** — the classification leans on the deterministic per-question scores and bands you set, so the segment follows fixed thresholds rather than AI judgement. Best when you need predictability.

**Result visibility** controls whether the respondent sees their result:

- **Show result** — the segment/score is revealed (quiz or profile style).
- **Hidden (routes only)** — the segment shapes the outcome silently; the respondent just sees a tailored result.

---

## Defining segments

Segments are the named profiles your scoring produces. You add them in the **Result Segments** section of the Scoring Node settings. Each segment has:

- **A name** — descriptive and internally meaningful (e.g. *"Growth-Ready"*, *"Not Yet Ready"*, *"Strong Fit"*).
- **A colour** — for at-a-glance identification in the canvas and analytics.
- **A description** — what this profile represents. In Automatic mode this is what the AI uses to make accurate assignments, so write it richly.
- **An output instruction** — how the outcome should be tailored for this segment. This is prepended to the Report / Document / Offer generation prompt, so a *"Growth-Ready"* segment can get a forward-looking, action-first report while a *"Needs Foundation Work"* segment gets one that meets them where they are.

## Adding it to the canvas

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Powers tab">
    In the floating toolbar at the bottom of the canvas, click the **Powers** tab and find the **Scoring** node.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drag it onto the canvas">
    Position it between your AI Product node and your Outcome node.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the nodes in sequence">
    Draw a connection from your **AI Product** node to the **Scoring** node, then from the **Scoring** node to your **Outcome** node: AI Product → Scoring → Outcome. The Scoring node must be a direct neighbour of the AI Product node.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure it">
    Click the Scoring node to open its settings. Write your objective, choose Automatic or Rule-based, set result visibility, and add your segments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Scoring objectives

The **objective** frames what the scoring is optimising for:

| Objective | Use when you want to evaluate… |
|---|---|
| **Lead Fit** | How well the person matches your ideal client profile |
| **Readiness** | Whether they're ready to act, buy, or move forward now |
| **Diagnosis** | What category of problem or situation they're in |
| **Recommendation** | Which approach, tier, or solution best fits their situation |
| **Offer Match** | Which of your offers, packages, or services fits them best |
| **Custom** | Something specific to your product — define your own framing |

## Why this replaces conditional logic

Every other form builder solves "different people need different paths" with **conditional logic** — explicit *if/then* rules you author and maintain. The Scoring Node solves the same problem with a fundamentally different model.

| | Conditional logic (other tools) | Productised Scoring |
|---|---|---|
| You define… | the **routes** (if/then rules) | the **destinations** (segments) |
| Operates on… | exact answer values | the meaning of the whole conversation |
| Scope | one field per rule | holistic — all answers, free text, and nuance |
| Maintenance | grows combinatorially as cases multiply | flat — N segments, no rule tree |
| What it changes | which questions/pages appear | what the AI actually generates for the outcome |

Conditional logic does two jobs: branch the **questions**, and branch the **outcome**. Productised covers both **without rules**:

- The **AI Product node** already adapts its questions conversationally — it asks the right follow-ups based on what someone says, so you never build skip-logic for the intake.
- The **Scoring Node** adapts the **outcome** — it classifies the person and tailors what's generated for them.

So the maze of branching screens collapses into: *adaptive conversation in → one classification → personalised outcome out.*

<Callout kind="note">
**Where you still want fixed rules:** deterministic gating — "budget under £X is always disqualified", compliance thresholds, anything you must be able to audit. That's exactly what the deterministic per-question scoring and Rule-based mode are for. You get AI judgement for nuance *and* fixed rules for hard gates — in the same node.
</Callout>

## What a scored result looks like in the data

Each completed response shows its assigned segment alongside the standard response data. Where deterministic scoring is enabled, you'll also see the per-question scores and overall total that fed the result — so you can review how your scoring is performing and spot patterns across your leads. Use **`get_segment_breakdown`** in Claude (or the Analytics tab) to see how respondents are distributed across segments.
