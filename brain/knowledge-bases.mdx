There are two places knowledge lives in Productised — and they serve different purposes.

The **Knowledge Power** is per-product. You attach it to a specific AI product on the canvas, and it grounds that product's conversations and outputs in your documents. It works at the product level.

The **Brain Knowledge Base** is workspace-level. It's the context Brain draws on across all your strategic conversations — not just one product, but everything. Upload your positioning, your methodology, your market research, and Brain will use it every time you ask it something.

Same underlying capability. Completely different scope.

---

## What to upload

Brain's Knowledge Base is for strategic context — the kind of material that shapes how you think about your business and your market. Good sources to upload:

- Positioning documents and value proposition frameworks
- Your methodology or proprietary process
- Market research and competitor analysis
- Frameworks and models you use with clients
- Case studies and client outcome summaries
- Pricing guides and offer structures
- Any document that captures how you think about your niche

If it would help a strategic advisor understand your business better, it belongs in the Knowledge Base.

---

## The Sources tab

The left panel in Brain includes a **Sources** tab listing all the documents and URLs you've added. Each entry shows the file name and character count. You can remove individual sources at any time.

---

## How to add a source

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Sources tab">
    In the Brain left panel, select the **Sources** tab.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Upload a file or add a URL">
    Drag and drop a file onto the upload area, or click to browse. Supported file types: PDF, TXT, MD, CSV.

    To add a web page, paste the URL into the URL field and confirm. Brain will scrape and index the page automatically.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for indexing">
    Each source is processed and indexed after upload. Once indexed, Brain can draw on it in any future conversation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## How Brain uses your sources

When you ask Brain a question, it doesn't read every document from start to finish. It vector-searches your sources for the chunks most relevant to what you're asking, and weaves that context into its response.

You don't need to tell Brain which document to look in. You don't need to reference sources by name. Brain finds the relevant parts automatically, based on what your question is about.

The practical effect: the more relevant material you have in your Knowledge Base, the more grounded and specific Brain's advice becomes. A Brain that knows your methodology will give better product recommendations than one working from a blank slate.
