title: Memory

Every time you have a conversation with Brain, it's paying attention to more than your question. It's noting the things that matter — your niche, your audience, the products you've discussed, your goals — and saving them for next time.

That's Brain's memory system. And it means that from the second conversation onwards, Brain already knows who you are.

---

## What Brain remembers

Brain automatically extracts and saves key facts from your conversations. Things like:

- Your target audience and niche
- The products you've built or discussed
- Strategic goals you've mentioned
- Constraints or preferences you've expressed (e.g. "I don't want to add more complexity to my funnel right now")
- Funnel gaps or opportunities that came up

You don't configure any of this. Brain handles it automatically in the background.

---

## How memories shape future conversations

Saved memories are used to personalise every subsequent conversation. Brain doesn't start from zero. It starts from what it already knows about your business — and that context shapes every suggestion it makes.

If you told Brain last month that you work with executive coaches who have established practices and aren't looking for mass-market lead generation, that's still true next month. Brain won't suggest a high-volume ToFU quiz lead magnet when it knows your audience is narrow and premium.

The practical effect: your conversations get sharper over time. The more you use Brain, the more precisely it can advise you.

---

## The Memories tab

The left panel in Brain includes a **Memories** tab showing the context Brain has saved from your conversations. Each entry shows the fact or insight Brain captured.

If a memory is out of date or no longer accurate — for example, you've pivoted your niche, or a product you were considering is no longer relevant — you can delete it. Brain will stop using that context in future conversations.

<Callout kind="info">Memories are there to serve you, not bind you. If something Brain saved is no longer true, delete it. Brain will adapt.</Callout>

---

## Memories are workspace-wide

Memories belong to your workspace, not to a single user. Everyone on your team benefits from the same accumulated context. If a collaborator has had conversations with Brain that established useful context about your audience or positioning, that context is available in your conversations too.

This is especially useful for teams where different people handle different parts of the strategy — Brain maintains a coherent picture of the workspace regardless of who built it up.

---

## How memory retrieval works

Brain uses vector search to match memories to your current conversation. When you ask a question, Brain doesn't load every saved memory into the conversation — it finds the most relevant ones, based on semantic similarity to what you're discussing, and surfaces only those.

The result is context that feels natural rather than cluttered. Brain brings in what's relevant without flooding the conversation with information that isn't.
